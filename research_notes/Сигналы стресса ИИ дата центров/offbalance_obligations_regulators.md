## 1. The Fed source for the ">$3 trillion" figure: where published, wording, which seven companies, what is counted, horizon, on- vs off-balance-sheet, and the risk the Fed draws

### Takeaway
The claim is traceable to two Fed documents: the **Financial Stability Report dated 13 November 2026** (new Box 2.2 "Leverage in the Data Center Buildout", pp. 23–25) and **Governor Michael Barr's speech of 16 November 2026**, which put the "potential maximum nominal value" of the off-balance-sheet obligations "above $3 trillion"; the seven firms are Amazon, Alphabet, Broadcom, Meta, Microsoft, Nvidia and Oracle, data through Q2 2026, split into >$1 trillion of leases signed but not yet commenced and >$2 trillion of other commitments (take-or-pay contracts, residual value guarantees), all undiscounted and "not directly comparable" with debt. **Verification status: confirmed by the existence of the primary URLs (FSR PDF, Barr speech, FOMC minutes on federalreserve.gov) and by consistent wording across Fortune, Axios and many secondary sites; NOT verified by reading the Fed PDF itself (fetch blocked)** — in particular it is unverified whether the FSR itself prints a single "$3 trillion" total or only the two components.

### Inferences
- The FSR appears to publish two components (>$1 trillion leases not yet commenced; >$2 trillion other commitments); the single ">$3 trillion" appears to be Barr's summation ("potential maximum nominal value") and the press's. Safest wording for the report: "more than $3 trillion in undiscounted, maximum nominal off-balance-sheet obligations (Fed FSR, 13 Nov 2026; Barr, 16 Nov 2026)".
- The Fed's split (>$1tn + >$2tn, Q2 2026) matches the WSJ's 17 Aug 2026 filings tally ($1.2tn uncommenced leases + $1.9tn purchase commitments), so the Fed staff number is most likely an aggregation of 10-Q/10-K footnote disclosures (leases not yet commenced, purchase obligations, guarantees) for a narrower group (seven firms, without SpaceX and AMD) rather than a new supervisory dataset. This is an inference from matching numbers, not a statement in the Fed text.

### Inferences
- The authorities' own framing implies a hybrid analogy: the *asset* side resembles the telecom/fiber cycle (long-lived shells, short-lived equipment, capex racing ahead of proven demand), while the *liability* side has acquired 2007-style features (off-balance-sheet vehicles, guarantees by sponsors, tranching sold to funds and insurers, opacity in private credit). The ECB's logic — dot-com was shallow because it was equity-financed — is the key reason regulators now focus on the debt shift rather than on valuations alone.
- The strongest counter-argument in the retrieved material is structural, not optimistic: obligors are a handful of very profitable firms that "may absorb losses", the debt directly behind the boom is still small relative to the system (Barr), and banks' direct exposure is limited. The weak points regulators identify are exactly where that argument does not reach: weaker counterparties (neoclouds, AI labs, developers), residual-value and lease guarantees that convert third-party failures into hyperscaler liabilities, and non-bank holders of subordinated risk.
- The "contracted demand" defence is double-edged in the IMF framing: the $1.4tn of contracted revenue is owed by the same firms that received the $300bn of equity and $650bn of debt, so contract quality depends on the counterparties' continued access to funding.
- The negative free cash flow of most US hyperscalers in 2026 (ECB) weakens the "cash-rich tenants fund it from cash flow" argument relative to 2024–25.

### Gaps
- No independent analyst comparisons (sell-side research, academics, economic historians, short-sellers, credit strategists) were researched in this session: specific 2008 and 1998–2001 parallels (e.g., SIV/conduit analogies, vendor financing, capacity swaps/IRUs, dark fiber utilisation), quantified capex-to-GDP comparisons, and named counter-arguments (equity cushions in SPVs, lease structures, take-or-pay enforceability, bank exposure estimates) remain to be sourced.
- Construction-delay risk (power interconnection, equipment lead times, tenantless builds) was captured only through the BoE probe and the Bayes report; no US evidence (delays at named campuses, lease commencement slippage, liquidated-damages clauses) was retrieved.
- The quantitative basis of the Fed's telecom comparison (which metric, which years) and the ECB's "2.7x dot-com share" headline were not verified.
