## 1. Nvidia–OpenAI Ohio guarantee: which project, what is guaranteed, trigger conditions, what is not covered during construction, who reported it, what the parties say

### Takeaway
The claim in the brief is confirmed in substance by Nvidia's own filings (as rendered in search results): on 17 Aug 2026 Nvidia signed guarantees capped at $105 billion that give credit support for about 4.25 GW (nine buildings) of SB Energy's PORTS-Pike campus in Pike County, Ohio, leased to OpenAI for 20 years, and each guarantee "generally becomes effective upon commencement of the applicable lease", conditional on SB Energy meeting ready-for-service (RFS) conditions — first expected in Nvidia's fiscal 2029 (calendar 2028). On 17 Nov 2026 interim CEO/CFO Colette Kress said on the record that if a build is delayed "our guarantee doesn't exist anymore"; Nvidia says it has made no payments under any guarantee. The construction lenders, the RFS definition, cure periods and outside dates were not found.

### Inferences
- The final August structure is narrower than the July report. WSJ's July description had the guarantee covering "the lease and construction debt"; the signed instruments, as Nvidia describes them, attach per building at lease commencement after RFS. Read together with Kress's statement, this means Nvidia carries no (or almost no) construction-period risk at PORTS-Pike: before a building is ready for service, the money at risk belongs to SB Energy's equity holders (SoftBank, OpenAI and Nvidia's own $1.5 billion stake) and to whoever lends for construction. This is an inference from timing language, not a statement found in a source.
- The "first guarantee in fiscal 2029" and "capacity online from calendar 2028" statements are consistent with each other: Nvidia's fiscal 2029 starts on 31 Jan 2028 (date given only in the Perera post).
- If the Perera reading is right, the per-building guarantee documents may still contain a pre-RFS payment mechanic ("Pre-RFS Termination Payment"). Until the exhibit is read, the correct wording for a report is "Nvidia says the guarantees are not effective before RFS; one unverified reading of the filed Building 1 guarantee points to a separately defined, redacted pre-RFS termination payment".
- Because the instrument is a residual-value / shortfall guarantee with a cap that amortises as OpenAI pays rent, $105 billion is a ceiling reached only if all nine buildings are delivered and OpenAI then defaults early; it is zero today.

### Gaps
- Construction lenders / financing banks for PORTS-Pike: not identified in any search result. Whether project debt has been raised at all is unknown.
- Exact definitions: "ready-for-service conditions", the guaranteed minimum value, the "satisfactory credit rating" test, cure periods, outside dates, termination rights and any liquidated damages are not available in search snippets (one source notes they were known only from the company's own summary in August). The 10-Q for the quarter ended 25 Oct 2026 and its exhibits were not surfaced by search; only the CFO commentary 8-K was.
- No direct on-record OpenAI or SB Energy/SoftBank comment on the construction-period gap was found.
- The 19 Nov 2026 "Building 1 guarantee" filing and the "Pre-RFS Termination Payment" term rest on one Substack post — unverified.
- Whether Nvidia has insured part of the exposure: Crypto Briefing reports about $12.9 billion of insurance cover tied to Nvidia's infrastructure commitments, but another piece from the same outlet says the figure appears to relate to aggregate guarantees rather than an insurance policy, and no filing or company statement was found — treat as unverified ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/nvidia-13b-insurance-ai-infrastructure/)).
- The publication date and text of the WSJ item saying Kress cut the guarantee from $250 billion to $105 billion were not retrieved (known only through Yahoo Finance/TIKR and FourWeekMBA).

## 2. Other Nvidia credit support: lease guarantees, capacity backstops, rent-back commitments, investments, SPVs, total contingent exposure

### Takeaway
At 25 Oct 2026 Nvidia reports $118 billion of maximum gross exposure under guarantees ($105 billion SB Energy/OpenAI, $3.5 billion land-power-shell lease guarantees for neoclouds, about $9.5 billion of power-and-systems credit support for unnamed partners) plus $32 billion of contingent "neocloud agreements" (down from $36 billion in July) — together the roughly $150 billion that IFR says Nvidia could walk away from if projects slip. Counting everything Nvidia has promised to buy, back or invest, Yahoo Finance arrives at nearly $670 billion (Hunterbrook: $530.5 billion as of July), most of it chip-supply commitments. Older anchors are the $6.3 billion CoreWeave unsold-capacity order (Sept 2025, runs to 13 Apr 2032) and the $860.3 million Nscale lease guarantee (Oct 2025).

- Nvidia now uses four distinct risk-transfer forms: (a) residual-value / lease guarantees for the building shell (SB Energy, Nscale, "$3.5bn LPS"), (b) power-and-systems credit support ($9.5bn, new in Q3), (c) demand backstops — buying or renting back unsold GPU capacity (CoreWeave $6.3bn; July 2026 neocloud programme $32–36bn), (d) master leases it signs itself and intends to reassign (~$20bn). - The $860.3 million Nscale/Ward County guarantee is small but is the template: guarantee limited to an initial period (five years), paid for with warrants, partially cash-collateralised by escrow.
- The fall in neocloud agreements from $36bn to $32bn in one quarter, together with the new "could be reduced or eliminated" language, suggests at least some arrangements were cut, lapsed or re-scoped — the reason is not stated in any snippet.

## 3. Backstops from other tenants and vendors: Google (Fluidstack / TeraWulf / Cipher / Hut 8), Meta Hyperion, Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle, SoftBank, AMD / Broadcom / Dell / Supermicro, chip-collateralised loans

### Takeaway

### Inferences
- The Google structures and the Nvidia structures share the same architecture: an investment-grade party supports the obligations of a sub-investment-grade tenant (Fluidstack, Anthropic, OpenAI, Nscale) toward a developer-landlord, takes equity-linked compensation (warrants, exclusivity), and is on the hook only after the lease has commenced. The supporter underwrites tenant credit risk, not construction or delivery risk.
- Google's backstops are termination-fee / step-in mechanisms that amortise (Abernathy: $1.3bn falling to $742m by 2031), so the "headline backstop" overstates protection late in the lease term in the same way that the "$105bn" overstates Nvidia's exposure early on.
- Adding the disclosed caps gives about $6.2bn of Google backstops at TeraWulf (Lake Mariner + Abernathy) and Cipher ($3.2bn + $1.3bn + $1.73bn), before the uncapped Hut 8 and TeraWulf-Kentucky commitments (my arithmetic; no source gives a total).
